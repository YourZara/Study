2. 2's complement representation; BCD addition 23 + 48 [1+2]

2's complement: a signed-number representation in which a positive number is written in ordinary binary (MSB = 0) and a negative number is written as the 2's complement of its magnitude (MSB = 1). 2's complement = 1's complement (invert all bits) + 1. Example (8-bit): +5 = 00000101; 1's complement = 11111010; add 1 → −5 = 11111011. It has only one zero and addition/subtraction use the same adder.

BCD addition 23 + 48

   23 = 0010 0011
 + 48 = 0100 1000
 ------------------
 Binary sum  0110 1011
 Low nibble 1011 (= 11 > 9) → invalid, add correction 0110
   1011 + 0110 = 1 0001  → digit 0001, carry 1 to upper nibble
 Upper nibble: 0110 + 1 = 0111 (valid, ≤ 9)
 Result = 0111 0001
23 + 48 = (0111 0001)BCD = 71

3. De Morgan's theorems with truth table and diagrams [4]

Theorem 1: The complement of a product equals the sum of the complements: (A·B)' = A' + B'.
Theorem 2: The complement of a sum equals the product of the complements: (A + B)' = A'·B'.
They extend to any number of variables: (ABC…)' = A'+B'+C'+… and (A+B+C+…)' = A'B'C'….

Columns (AB)' and A'+B' are identical, and (A+B)' and A'B' are identical, so both theorems are proved.

Fig 1: NAND gate ≡ OR gate with inverted inputs (bubbled OR): (AB)' = A'+B'

Fig 2: NOR gate ≡ AND gate with inverted inputs (bubbled AND): (A+B)' = A'B'

5. Magnitude comparator; 3-bit magnitude comparator [1+5]

Magnitude comparator: a combinational circuit that compares two binary numbers A and B and gives three outputs: A > B, A = B, A < B (only one is 1 at a time).

Design of 3-bit comparator (A = A2A1A0, B = B2B1B0)

1-bit comparison (Ai vs Bi): A>B = AiBi', A<B = Ai'Bi, A=B = Ai⊙Bi = AiBi + Ai'Bi'. Define equality of each bit xi = Ai ⊙ Bi.

Comparison starts from the MSB: if the MSBs differ, the result is decided; if equal, compare the next bit, and so on.

(A=B) = x2·x1·x0
(A>B) = A2B2' + x2·A1B1' + x2·x1·A0B0'
(A<B) = A2'B2 + x2·A1'B1 + x2·x1·A0'B0

Fig: 3-bit comparator – xi are three XNOR gates (Ai, Bi); A>B and A<B from AND–OR networks; A=B from a 3-input AND.

8. Types of shift registers; Johnson counter [2+3]

A shift register is a group of flip-flops connected in cascade that stores binary data and shifts it left or right on each clock pulse. Types (by data entry/exit):

  • SISO – serial in, serial out: delay line / data storage.
  • SIPO – serial in, parallel out: serial-to-parallel conversion.
  • PISO – parallel in, serial out: parallel-to-serial conversion (needs Load/Shift control).
  • PIPO – parallel in, parallel out: temporary storage.
  • Bidirectional (shift left/right) and Universal register (parallel load + both shifts).

Johnson (twisted-ring) counter

A Johnson counter is a shift register whose inverted output Q' of the last flip-flop is fed back to the input D of the first flip-flop. An n-bit Johnson counter has 2n states (mod-2n); a 4-bit one is mod-8. Starting from 0000, 1s are shifted in from the left until the register is full (1111), then 0s are shifted in until 0000 again.

Fig: 4-bit Johnson counter (D flip-flops, common clock, Q'D → DA).

Fig: timing diagram – each output is a square wave of frequency f/8 shifted by one clock period from the previous one. Only 2-input gates are needed to decode any state (e.g. 0000 = A'D', 1000 = AB', 1111 = AD, 0111 = A'B).

9. 3-bit ripple up counter with positive-edge triggered clock [5]

Circuit: three JK flip-flops with J = K = 1 (toggle FFs). The external clock drives FF-A (LSB) only. Because the FFs are positive-edge triggered, the clock of each following FF is taken from the Q' output of the previous FF: a falling edge of Q (= rising edge of Q') then toggles the next FF, which gives up-counting. Q'A → CLK of FF-B, Q'B → CLK of FF-C. The FFs do not change simultaneously – the change ripples through (asynchronous counter).

Fig: 3-bit asynchronous up counter (J = K = 1).

Fig: timing diagram (ideal, propagation delay of each FF ignored). QA toggles at every rising clock edge (f/2), QB at every falling edge of QA (f/4), QC at every falling edge of QB (f/8).

Points: mod-8 counter, counts 000→111 then recycles to 000 (divide-by-8); total delay = 3 × tpd, which limits the maximum clock frequency.

12. Short note on frequency counter [3]

A frequency counter measures the frequency of an unknown periodic signal by counting the number of its cycles in a precisely known time interval (gate time T): f = N / T.

Input signal fx→Amplifier / Schmitt trigger
(pulse shaper)→AND gate→Decade counter(s)→Latch→Decoder + display
Crystal oscillator→Divider chain (decades)→Gate-control flip-flop
(gate time T = 1 s, 0.1 s…)→ enable to AND gate; also generates latch & reset pulses

Operation: the Schmitt trigger converts the input into clean pulses. The stable crystal time base is divided down to give a gate pulse of exactly T (e.g. 1 s). While the gate is open, the AND gate passes the pulses to the counter. When the gate closes, the count N is transferred to a latch, displayed, and the counter is reset for the next measurement. Example: N = 4500 in T = 1 s → f = 4.5 kHz; with T = 10 ms the display would show the frequency in units of 100 Hz. Accuracy depends on the crystal stability and on ±1 count error.
