DC Machine (Generators & Motors)

Constructional details, commutator action, generator EMF and motor torque derivations, self-regulating role of back EMF, 3-point starters, and speed control methods.

8.1 & 8.2 Constructional Details & Governing Equations

Major Constructional Components

  • Yoke (Magnetic Frame): Outer cast steel or cast iron housing that provides mechanical protection and carries magnetic flux for the pole cores.
  • Pole Core & Pole Shoes: Laminated steel projecting inward to carry field coils. Pole shoes spread magnetic flux uniformly across the air gap and support the coils.
  • Armature Core: Slotted cylinder made of laminated silicon steel sheets to carry insulated armature conductors while minimizing eddy current losses.
  • Armature Windings:
    • Lap Winding: Coils overlap each other. Number of parallel paths \(A = P\). Suitable for high current, low voltage machines.
    • Wave Winding: Coils progress forward in a wave. Number of parallel paths \(A = 2\) always. Suitable for high voltage, low current machines.
  • Commutator: Cylindrical assembly of wedge-shaped copper segments insulated by mica. In generators, acts as a mechanical rectifier converting AC induced EMF into DC terminal voltage. In motors, reverses current in each coil as it crosses magnetic neutral zone to maintain unidirectional torque.
  • Carbon Brushes: Spring-loaded carbon/graphite blocks riding on the commutator surface to conduct current between the rotating armature and stationary external terminals.

Governing EMF and Torque Derivations

\[\text{Generator EMF: } E_g = \frac{P \, \Phi \, Z \, N}{60 A} \text{ [Volts]}\] \[\text{Motor Torque: } T = \frac{P \, \Phi \, Z \, I_a}{2\pi A} \approx 0.159 \frac{P Z}{A} \Phi I_a \text{ [N}\cdot\text{m]}\]
Where: \(P\) = poles, \(\Phi\) = flux per pole [Wb], \(Z\) = total conductors, \(N\) = speed [rpm], \(A\) = parallel paths (\(A=P\) for lap, \(A=2\) for wave), \(I_a\) = armature current [A].
(See full derivation in Derivation Sheet Section 3).

8.3 & 8.4 DC Motor Types, Back EMF & Performance Curves

The Self-Regulating Role of Back EMF

When a DC motor rotates, its armature conductors cut the field flux, inducing a counter-EMF (Back EMF \(E_b\)) that opposes the applied terminal voltage: \[E_b = \frac{P \Phi Z N}{60 A} = V - I_a R_a\] Armature current: \[I_a = \frac{V - E_b}{R_a}\] Why \(E_b\) acts as an automatic governor:
1. If mechanical load on the motor increases, the motor momentarily slows down (\(N \downarrow\)).
2. Because \(E_b \propto N\), back EMF decreases (\(E_b \downarrow\)).
3. This allows armature current to rise automatically: \(I_a = \frac{V - E_b}{R_a} \uparrow\).
4. Torque \(T \propto \Phi I_a\) increases to match the new mechanical load demand until equilibrium is restored!

Performance Characteristics Comparison

Machine Type Torque vs Current (\(T\) vs \(I_a\)) Speed vs Current (\(N\) vs \(I_a\)) Standard Applications
DC Shunt Motor Linear: \(T \propto I_a\) (since \(\Phi\) is constant). Almost constant speed with slight drop (5–8%) due to \(I_a R_a\) drop. Known as a "constant-speed motor". Lathes, centrifugal pumps, fans, machine tools.
DC Series Motor Parabolic before saturation: \(T \propto I_a^2\). Produces huge starting torque! Hyperbolic: \(N \propto \frac{1}{I_a}\). Speed becomes dangerously high at no-load! Must never be started on no-load. Electric trains/traction, cranes, elevators, hoists.
Cumulative Compound Higher starting torque than shunt due to aiding series field. Moderate speed drop under load without runaway danger at light load. Rolling mills, heavy punching presses, shears.

8.5 & 8.6 Starting of DC Motors & Speed Control

Why Starters are Essential & 3-Point Starter Operation

At standstill (\(t = 0\)), rotor speed \(N = 0 \implies E_b = 0\). If direct line voltage is applied: \[I_{\text{start}} = \frac{V - 0}{R_a} = \frac{V}{R_a}\] Since armature resistance \(R_a\) is very small (\(< 0.5\,\Omega\)), direct switching draws 15–25 times rated full-load current, blowing fuses, melting insulation, and burning commutator segments.

3-Point Starter Protective Functions

• Starting Resistor: Arranged in tapped studs to introduce high resistance at start, cut out step-by-step as the motor accelerates and builds \(E_b\).
• No-Volt Coil (NVC): Electromagnet connected in series with the shunt field. Holds the starter handle in the 'ON' position. If power fails or the field circuit breaks, NVC de-energizes and a spring pulls the arm back to 'OFF', preventing dangerous re-energization.
• Overload Release (OLR) Coil: Connected in series with the main supply. If excessive current flows, OLR lifts an iron armature that short-circuits the NVC, immediately tripping the starter handle.

Speed Control Methods

From the basic speed equation: \[N = \frac{V - I_a R_a}{k \Phi}\]

Method Operational Mechanism Speed Range Drawback / Trade-off
Armature Resistance Control Add variable resistance \(R_{\text{ext}}\) in series with armature. Increases circuit resistance: \(E_b = V - I_a(R_a + R_{\text{ext}})\) drops. Below rated base speed only Poor efficiency (huge \(I_a^2 R_{\text{ext}}\) power waste in controller resistor); poor speed regulation.
Field Flux Control Insert variable rheostat into shunt field circuit. Reduces field current \(I_{sh}\), reducing flux \(\Phi\). Because \(N \propto 1/\Phi\), motor speeds up! Above rated base speed only Very high efficiency (field current is small, \(< 2\%\) of rated); limited at upper speeds by mechanical centrifugal limits and commutation sparking.

Master Worked Example: Speed Control with Added Armature Resistance

DC Shunt Motor Speed Control Numerical PYQ 2081 Ashwin Q8 (6 Marks)
Given:
Supply voltage \(V = 240\text{ V}\)
Armature resistance \(R_a = 0.4\,\Omega\)
Field resistance \(R_{sh} = 120\,\Omega\)
Initial current \(I_{L1} = 30\text{ A}\), Initial speed \(N_1 = 1400\text{ rpm}\)
Added series resistance \(R_{\text{ext}} = 1.2\,\Omega\)
Load torque is reduced by 20% \(\implies T_2 = 0.8 T_1\)
Calculations:
1. Field current (remains constant): \[I_{sh} = \frac{V}{R_{sh}} = \frac{240}{120} = 2\text{ A}\] 2. Initial armature current: \[I_{a1} = I_{L1} - I_{sh} = 30 - 2 = 28\text{ A}\] 3. Initial back EMF: \[E_{b1} = V - I_{a1} R_a = 240 - (28 \times 0.4) = 240 - 11.2 = 228.8\text{ V}\] 4. Torque relation (\(\Phi = \text{constant} \implies T \propto I_a\)): \[\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{I_{a2}}{I_{a1}} = 0.8 \implies I_{a2} = 0.8 \times 28 = 22.4\text{ A}\] 5. Total new armature resistance: \[R_{a2} = R_a + R_{\text{ext}} = 0.4 + 1.2 = 1.6\,\Omega\] 6. New back EMF: \[E_{b2} = V - I_{a2} R_{a2} = 240 - (22.4 \times 1.6) = 240 - 35.84 = 204.16\text{ V}\] 7. New speed: \[\frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{E_{b2}}{E_{b1}} = \frac{204.16}{228.8} \approx 0.8923\] \[N_2 = 1400 \times 0.8923 \approx 1249.2\text{ rpm}\]
Final Answer: Motor speed reduces from 1400 rpm to \(1249.2\text{ rpm}\).
