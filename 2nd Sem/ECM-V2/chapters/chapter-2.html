Transient Analysis of R-L-C Circuits by Classical Method

Time-domain differential equations, complementary functions, particular integrals, and complete dynamic solutions for first-order and second-order linear circuits.

2.1 & 2.2 First-Order Differential Equations & General Solution Structure

The Classical Complete Solution Principle

In the classical time-domain method, any linear circuit subjected to excitation is modeled by a non-homogeneous differential equation with constant coefficients: \[a_n \frac{d^n x}{dt^n} + \dots + a_1 \frac{dx}{dt} + a_0 x = f(t)\] The complete response \(x(t)\) is strictly the superposition of two components: \[x(t) = x_c(t) + x_p(t)\]

Component Mathematical Name Physical Engineering Meaning Key Characteristic
\(x_c(t)\) Complementary Function Transient / Natural Response: Energy exchange between circuit elements decaying to zero due to resistance. Depends strictly on internal network structure (\(R, L, C\)), independent of source waveform.
\(x_p(t)\) Particular Integral Forced / Steady-State Response: Sustained output dictated by the external excitation as \(t \to \infty\). Takes the same mathematical functional form as the forcing function \(f(t)\).

Method of Undetermined Coefficients (PI Selection Rules)

Excitation Type \(f(t)\) Assumed Form of Particular Integral \(x_p(t)\)
Constant DC: \(V_0\) or \(I_0\) \(K\) (constant)
Exponential: \(V_0 e^{-at}\) (where \(-a\) is not a natural root) \(K e^{-at}\)
Exponential: \(V_0 e^{-at}\) (where \(-a\) is a simple natural root) \(K t e^{-at}\)
Sinusoidal: \(V_m \cos(\omega t + \theta)\) or \(\sin(\omega t)\) \(A \cos(\omega t) + B \sin(\omega t)\)

2.3 Response of R-L and R-C Circuits (DC, Exponential, Sinusoidal)

1. DC Step Excitation

\[\text{Series RL: } i(t) = \frac{V}{R}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = \frac{L}{R}\] \[\text{Series RC: } v_C(t) = V\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad i(t) = \frac{V}{R} e^{-t/\tau}, \quad \tau = RC\]
Time Constant Physical Significance: In one time constant (\(t = \tau\)), the response reaches \(1 - e^{-1} \approx 63.2\%\) of its final steady-state value. The transient effectively dies out after \(5\tau\) (\(99.3\%\) settled).

2. Exponential Excitation

For a series RC circuit excited by \(v(t) = V_0 e^{-at}\), the complete solution derived via classical ODE is: \[i(t) = \frac{V_0}{R(1 - a RC)} \left[ e^{-t/RC} - a RC \, e^{-at} \right]\] (See full derivation in Derivation Sheet Section 5).

3. Sinusoidal AC Excitation

When series RL is excited by \(v(t) = V_m \sin(\omega t + \theta)\) switched on at \(t = 0\): \[L \frac{di}{dt} + R i = V_m \sin(\omega t + \theta)\] Particular integral: \[i_p(t) = \frac{V_m}{Z} \sin(\omega t + \theta - \phi), \quad \text{where } Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (\omega L)^2}, \quad \phi = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega L}{R}\right)\] Complementary function: \(i_c(t) = A e^{-Rt/L}\). Using initial condition \(i(0^+) = 0\): \[i(0^+) = A + \frac{V_m}{Z} \sin(\theta - \phi) = 0 \implies A = -\frac{V_m}{Z} \sin(\theta - \phi)\] \[i(t) = -\frac{V_m}{Z} \sin(\theta - \phi) e^{-Rt/L} + \frac{V_m}{Z} \sin(\omega t + \theta - \phi)\]

DC Offset Transient: If the switch is closed at \(\theta - \phi = \pm 90^\circ\), the initial DC transient term is at its maximum (\(V_m/Z\)), causing the peak instantaneous current to reach nearly twice the normal peak (the doubling effect)!

2.4 Response of Series R-L-C Circuits (DC Excitation)

Applying KVL to a series RLC circuit excited by a constant voltage \(V\): \[L \frac{d^2 i}{dt^2} + R \frac{di}{dt} + \frac{1}{C} i = 0\] Characteristic equation: \[s^2 + \frac{R}{L} s + \frac{1}{LC} = 0 \iff s^2 + 2\alpha s + \omega_0^2 = 0\] Roots: \[s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2}, \quad \alpha = \frac{R}{2L}, \quad \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}\]

Damping Regime Mathematical Criterion Roots Nature Current Solution \(i(t)\)
Overdamped \(\alpha > \omega_0 \iff R > 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\) Real, unequal, negative (\(s_1 \ne s_2 < 0\)) \(i(t) = A_1 e^{s_1 t} + A_2 e^{s_2 t}\)
Critically Damped \(\alpha = \omega_0 \iff R = 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\) Real, equal, negative (\(s_1 = s_2 = -\alpha\)) \(i(t) = (A_1 + A_2 t) e^{-\alpha t}\)
Underdamped \(\alpha < \omega_0 \iff R < 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\) Complex conjugates (\(-\alpha \pm j\omega_d\)) \(i(t) = e^{-\alpha t}[A_1 \cos(\omega_d t) + A_2 \sin(\omega_d t)]\), \(\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2}\)
Master Numerical: Series RLC Classical Method PYQ 2082 Baishakh Q3
Given:
DC Source \(V = 100\text{ V}\) switched at \(t = 0\).
\(R = 2\,\Omega, \quad L = 0.5\text{H}, \quad C = 1\text{F}\). Uncharged initially.
Calculations:
1. Parameters: \[\alpha = \frac{R}{2L} = \frac{2}{2(0.5)} = 2\text{ Np/s}\] \[\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.5 \times 1}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414\text{ rad/s}\] Since \(\alpha > \omega_0\) (\(2 > 1.414\)), the circuit is OVERDAMPED!
2. Roots: \[s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2} = -2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 2} = -2 \pm \sqrt{2}\] \[s_1 = -2 + \sqrt{2} \approx -0.586, \quad s_2 = -2 - \sqrt{2} \approx -3.414\] General solution: \(i(t) = A_1 e^{s_1 t} + A_2 e^{s_2 t}\).
3. Initial Conditions: \[i(0^+) = 0 \implies A_1 + A_2 = 0 \implies A_2 = -A_1\] Across inductor at \(t = 0^+\): \[v_L(0^+) = V - R i(0^+) - v_C(0^+) = 100 - 0 - 0 = 100\text{ V}\] \[\frac{di}{dt}(0^+) = \frac{v_L(0^+)}{L} = \frac{100}{0.5} = 200\text{ A/s}\] Differentiating general solution: \[\frac{di}{dt}(0^+) = s_1 A_1 + s_2 A_2 = A_1(s_1 - s_2) = A_1[(-2+\sqrt{2}) - (-2-\sqrt{2})] = A_1(2\sqrt{2}) = 200\] \[A_1 = \frac{200}{2\sqrt{2}} = 50\sqrt{2} \approx 70.71\text{ A}, \quad A_2 = -50\sqrt{2}\text{ A}\]
Final Answer: \[i(t) = 50\sqrt{2} \left[ e^{(-2+\sqrt{2})t} - e^{(-2-\sqrt{2})t} \right]\text{ A} \quad (t \ge 0)\]

2.6 & 2.7 Parallel R-L-C Circuits & Comparison with Series

Applying KCL to a parallel RLC circuit fed by a source current \(I_s\): \[\frac{v}{R} + \frac{1}{L}\int v \, dt + C \frac{dv}{dt} = I_s\] Differentiating: \[C \frac{d^2 v}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{R} \frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{1}{L} v = \frac{dI_s}{dt} \implies \frac{d^2 v}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{RC} \frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{1}{LC} v = 0\] Characteristic equation: \(s^2 + 2\alpha s + \omega_0^2 = 0\), where: \[\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}, \quad \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}\]

Comparison Table: Series RLC vs. Parallel RLC

Feature / Parameter Series R-L-C Circuit Parallel R-L-C Circuit
Primary Variable Current \(i(t)\) (common to all elements) Voltage \(v(t)\) (common across all elements)
Damping Factor \(\alpha\) \(\alpha = \frac{R}{2L}\) \(\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}\)
Resonant Frequency \(\omega_0\) \(\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}\) \(\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}\) (Identical!)
Overdamped Condition \(R > 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\) \(R < \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\)
Critically Damped Condition \(R = 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\) \(R = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\)
Underdamped Condition \(R < 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\) \(R > \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}\)
Effect of Increasing \(R\) Increases damping (suppresses oscillations) Decreases damping (encourages oscillations)
