Question 1 [6 Marks]: Nodal Analysis with Dependent Source

Question Statement: Find the current in the 10V source using nodal analysis for the circuit with node voltages \(v_1, v_2, v_3\). A \(6\,\Omega\) resistor connects \(v_1\) and \(v_3\). A \(2\,\Omega\) resistor connects \(v_1\) to ground with downward current \(i = v_1 / 2\). A 10V independent voltage source connects between \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) (with '+' at \(v_2\), '-' at \(v_1\)). A \(4\,\Omega\) resistor connects \(v_2\) to ground. A dependent voltage source of \(5i\) connects between \(v_2\) and \(v_3\) (with '+' at \(v_2\), '-' at \(v_3\)), and a \(3\,\Omega\) resistor connects \(v_3\) to ground.

Step-by-Step Nodal Solution Chapter 1
Step 1: Constraint Equations & Supernodes
Control variable: \(i = \frac{v_1}{2}\).
Between \(v_1\) and \(v_2\): \(v_2 - v_1 = 10 \implies v_2 = v_1 + 10\).
Between \(v_2\) and \(v_3\): \(v_2 - v_3 = 5i = 5\left(\frac{v_1}{2}\right) = 2.5 v_1 \implies v_3 = v_2 - 2.5 v_1 = (v_1 + 10) - 2.5 v_1 = 10 - 1.5 v_1\).
Notice that nodes 1, 2, and 3 are all interconnected by ideal voltage sources (one independent, one dependent), forming a single combined supernode enclosed around nodes 1, 2, and 3!
Step 2: KCL at Supernode (Nodes 1-2-3)
Sum of all currents leaving the supernode to ground: \[\frac{v_1}{2} + \frac{v_2}{4} + \frac{v_3}{3} = 0\] (The \(6\,\Omega\) resistor connects between \(v_1\) and \(v_3\), which is strictly internal to the supernode, so its current cancels out in the supernode KCL!).
Step 3: Substitution & Algebraic Solution
Substitute \(v_2 = v_1 + 10\) and \(v_3 = 10 - 1.5 v_1\): \[\frac{v_1}{2} + \frac{v_1 + 10}{4} + \frac{10 - 1.5 v_1}{3} = 0\] Multiply entire equation by 12: \[6 v_1 + 3(v_1 + 10) + 4(10 - 1.5 v_1) = 0\] \[6 v_1 + 3 v_1 + 30 + 40 - 6 v_1 = 0 \implies 3 v_1 + 70 = 0 \implies v_1 = -\frac{70}{3} \approx -23.33\text{ V}\] Now compute \(v_2\) and \(v_3\): \[v_2 = v_1 + 10 = -\frac{70}{3} + \frac{30}{3} = -\frac{40}{3} \approx -13.33\text{ V}\] \[v_3 = 10 - 1.5\left(-\frac{70}{3}\right) = 10 + 35 = 45\text{ V}\] Control current: \(i = \frac{v_1}{2} = -\frac{35}{3} \approx -11.67\text{ A}\).
Step 4: Current in the 10V Source
Apply KCL strictly at Node 1 to find current \(I_{10V}\) flowing from node 1 to node 2: \[I_{10V} + \frac{v_1}{2} + \frac{v_1 - v_3}{6} = 0\] \[I_{10V} = -\left[\frac{-70/3}{2} + \frac{-70/3 - 45}{6}\right] = -\left[-\frac{35}{3} + \frac{-205/3}{6}\right] = -\left[-\frac{70}{6} - \frac{205}{18}\right] = \frac{210 + 205}{18} = \frac{415}{18} \approx 23.06\text{ A}\]
Final Answer: Node voltages are \(v_1 = -23.33\text{ V}\), \(v_2 = -13.33\text{ V}\), \(v_3 = 45.0\text{ V}\). The current flowing through the 10V source (from \(v_1\) to \(v_2\)) is \(23.06\text{ A}\).
Question 2 [5 Marks]: Initial Condition Values at \(t = 0^+\) (Repeated in 2081 Ashwin)

Question Statement: Obtain the values of \(i_1(0^+), i_2(0^+), \frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+), \frac{di_2}{dt}(0^+)\), if the switch is closed at \(t = 0\) in the circuit shown: A 10V DC source connected via a switch to mesh 1 containing an inductor \(L = 1\text{H}\) in the top branch, a shunt resistor of \(10\,\Omega\), and mesh 2 containing a series resistor \(10\,\Omega\) and capacitor \(C = 1\text{F}\).

Step-by-Step Initial Conditions Evaluation Chapter 1
Step 1: State at \(t = 0^-\)
Before \(t=0\), the switch was open and the circuit was unenergized: \[i_L(0^-) = 0 \text{ A}, \quad v_C(0^-) = 0 \text{ V}\]
Step 2: Switching Invariance at \(t = 0^+\)
Current through an inductor and voltage across a capacitor cannot change instantaneously: \[i_L(0^+) = i_L(0^-) = 0 \implies i_1(0^+) = 0\text{ A}\] \[v_C(0^+) = v_C(0^-) = 0\text{ V}\] At \(t=0^+\), the inductor is in series with mesh 1, so \(i_1(0^+) = 0\).
For mesh 2, KVL gives: \(10(i_2(0^+) - i_1(0^+)) + 10 i_2(0^+) + v_C(0^+) = 0\).
Since \(i_1(0^+) = 0\) and \(v_C(0^+) = 0\): \[20 i_2(0^+) = 0 \implies i_2(0^+) = 0\text{ A}\]
Step 3: Evaluating \(\frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+)\)
Write KVL for Mesh 1 for \(t > 0\): \[10 = L \frac{di_1}{dt} + 10(i_1 - i_2)\] At \(t = 0^+\), substitute \(L = 1\text{H}\), \(i_1(0^+) = 0\), \(i_2(0^+) = 0\): \[10 = 1 \cdot \frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+) + 10(0 - 0) \implies \frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+) = 10\text{ A/s}\]
Step 4: Evaluating \(\frac{di_2}{dt}(0^+)\)
Write KVL for Mesh 2 for \(t > 0\): \[10(i_2 - i_1) + 10 i_2 + v_C = 0 \implies 20 i_2 - 10 i_1 + v_C = 0\] Differentiate with respect to \(t\): \[20 \frac{di_2}{dt} - 10 \frac{di_1}{dt} + \frac{dv_C}{dt} = 0\] Since \(\frac{dv_C}{dt} = \frac{i_C}{C} = \frac{i_2}{C}\), at \(t = 0^+\): \[\frac{dv_C}{dt}(0^+) = \frac{i_2(0^+)}{1} = \frac{0}{1} = 0\text{ V/s}\] Substitute into differentiated KVL: \[20 \frac{di_2}{dt}(0^+) - 10(10) + 0 = 0 \implies 20 \frac{di_2}{dt}(0^+) = 100 \implies \frac{di_2}{dt}(0^+) = 5\text{ A/s}\]
Final Results: \[i_1(0^+) = 0\text{ A}, \quad i_2(0^+) = 0\text{ A}, \quad \frac{di_1}{dt}(0^+) = 10\text{ A/s}, \quad \frac{di_2}{dt}(0^+) = 5\text{ A/s}\]

Question 4 [6 Marks]: Laplace Transform Method for RL/RC Circuit

Question Statement: In the given circuit, switch is opened at \(t = 0\). Find the current and voltage across inductor using Laplace transform method. Circuit: 4V DC source connected in parallel with a \(1\text{F}\) capacitor, and a branch with inductor \(L = 0.5\text{H}\) in series with \(R = 1\,\Omega\).

Laplace Transform Solution Chapter 3
Step 1: Steady-State Initial Values at \(t = 0^-\)
Before \(t = 0\), the switch was closed for a long time. In DC steady state:
• Capacitor acts as open circuit: \(v_C(0^-) = 4\text{ V}\).
• Inductor acts as short circuit: \(i_L(0^-) = \frac{4\text{ V}}{1\,\Omega} = 4\text{ A}\).
Step 2: s-Domain Transformed Circuit for \(t > 0\)
At \(t = 0\), switch opens, disconnecting the 4V source. The circuit becomes a single parallel loop of capacitor discharging into the series \(R-L\) branch.
s-Domain representation:
• Capacitor: impedance \(\frac{1}{sC} = \frac{1}{s}\) with initial series voltage source \(\frac{v_C(0^-)}{s} = \frac{4}{s}\).
• Inductor: impedance \(sL = 0.5s\) with initial series voltage generator \(L i_L(0^-) = 0.5 \times 4 = 2\text{ V}\) opposing current drop.
• Resistor: \(R = 1\,\Omega\).
Step 3: Solving for \(I(s)\)
Applying KVL in the loop for clockwise current \(I(s)\): \[\frac{4}{s} - \frac{1}{s} I(s) - 1 \cdot I(s) - 0.5 s I(s) + 2 = 0\] \[I(s) \left( 0.5 s + 1 + \frac{1}{s} \right) = \frac{4}{s} + 2 = \frac{2s + 4}{s}\] \[I(s) \left( \frac{0.5 s^2 + s + 1}{s} \right) = \frac{2s + 4}{s} \implies I(s) = \frac{2s + 4}{0.5 s^2 + s + 1} = \frac{4(s + 2)}{s^2 + 2s + 2}\]
Step 4: Inverse Laplace Transform
Complete the square in denominator: \(s^2 + 2s + 2 = (s + 1)^2 + 1\). \[I(s) = \frac{4(s + 1) + 4}{(s + 1)^2 + 1} = 4 \frac{s + 1}{(s + 1)^2 + 1^2} + 4 \frac{1}{(s + 1)^2 + 1^2}\] Taking the inverse Laplace transform: \[i_L(t) = 4 e^{-t} \cos(t) + 4 e^{-t} \sin(t) = 4 e^{-t} (\cos t + \sin t)\text{ A} \quad (t \ge 0)\] Voltage across inductor: \[v_L(t) = L \frac{di_L}{dt} = 0.5 \frac{d}{dt}\left[ 4 e^{-t}(\cos t + \sin t) \right]\] \[\frac{di_L}{dt} = 4 \left[ -e^{-t}(\cos t + \sin t) + e^{-t}(-\sin t + \cos t) \right] = 4 e^{-t} (-2\sin t) = -8 e^{-t} \sin t\] \[v_L(t) = 0.5 (-8 e^{-t} \sin t) = -4 e^{-t} \sin(t)\text{ V}\]
Final Results: \[i_L(t) = 4 e^{-t} (\cos t + \sin t)\text{ A}, \quad v_L(t) = -4 e^{-t} \sin(t)\text{ V}\]
Question 5 [7 Marks]: Network Function & Asymptotic Bode Plot

Question Statement: What is a network function? Plot the asymptotic Bode graph of the transfer function: \[H(s) = \frac{10(s^2+8s+15)}{s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)}\]

Bode Plot Construction Chapter 4
Step 1: Factorization & Cancellation
Factor the numerator quadratic: \(s^2 + 8s + 15 = (s + 3)(s + 5)\). \[H(s) = \frac{10(s+3)(s+5)}{s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)} = \frac{10(s+5)}{s^2(0.05s+1)}\]
Step 2: Standard Time-Constant (Bode) Form
\[H(s) = \frac{10 \times 5 \left(1 + \frac{s}{5}\right)}{s^2 (1 + 0.05 s)} = \frac{50 \left(1 + \frac{s}{5}\right)}{s^2 \left(1 + \frac{s}{20}\right)}\] Bode gain \(K_B = 50\).
• Corner frequency for simple zero: \(\omega_{z1} = 5\text{ rad/s}\).
• Corner frequency for simple pole: \(\omega_{p1} = \frac{1}{0.05} = 20\text{ rad/s}\).
• Double pole at origin: \(1/s^2\).
Step 3: Magnitude Slope Table
Frequency Range (rad/s)Contributing FactorSlope ChangeNet Slope
\(\omega < 5\)Double pole at origin \(1/s^2\)Initial\(-40\text{ dB/decade}\)
\(5 \le \omega < 20\)Zero at \(\omega = 5\)\(+20\text{ dB/decade}\)\(-20\text{ dB/decade}\)
\(\omega \ge 20\)Pole at \(\omega = 20\)\(-20\text{ dB/decade}\)\(-40\text{ dB/decade}\)
Reference gain at \(\omega = 1\text{ rad/s}\): \[|H(j1)|_{\text{dB}} \approx 20\log_{10}(K_B) - 40\log_{10}(1) = 20\log_{10}(50) = 20(1.699) = 33.98\text{ dB}\] Gain at first corner frequency \(\omega = 5\): \[|H(j5)|_{\text{dB}} = 33.98 - 40\log_{10}(5) = 33.98 - 40(0.699) = 33.98 - 27.96 = 6.02\text{ dB}\]
Step 4: Phase Angle Equation
\[\phi(\omega) = -180^\circ + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{5}\right) - \tan^{-1}(0.05\omega)\]
Summary: Low-frequency slope is \(-40\text{ dB/dec}\); breaks at \(\omega = 5\text{ rad/s}\) to \(-20\text{ dB/dec}\); breaks at \(\omega = 20\text{ rad/s}\) back to \(-40\text{ dB/dec}\).
Question 6 [6 Marks]: Z-Parameters of Two-Port Network

Question Statement: Find Z parameter for given two port network and state whether network is symmetrical or reciprocal. Network structure: \(8\,\Omega\) resistor in series at Port 1, shunt resistor \(20\,\Omega\), series resistor \(10\,\Omega\), shunt resistor \(20\,\Omega\) across Port 2.

Z-Parameter Evaluation Chapter 5
Step 1: Open-Circuit at Port 2 (\(I_2 = 0\))
When port 2 is open:
Resistors \(10\,\Omega\) and \(20\,\Omega\) are in series (\(= 30\,\Omega\)), connected in parallel with the first \(20\,\Omega\) resistor: \[R_{p1} = 20 \parallel 30 = \frac{20 \times 30}{50} = 12\,\Omega\] Input impedance at Port 1: \[Z_{11} = \left.\frac{V_1}{I_1}\right|_{I_2 = 0} = 8 + R_{p1} = 8 + 12 = 20\,\Omega\] Voltage across the second \(20\,\Omega\) resistor: By voltage division across the parallel branch: \[V_2 = V_{20\Omega,\text{shunt}} \times \frac{20}{10 + 20} = (I_1 \times 12) \times \frac{2}{3} = 8 I_1 \implies Z_{21} = \left.\frac{V_2}{I_1}\right|_{I_2 = 0} = 8\,\Omega\]
Step 2: Open-Circuit at Port 1 (\(I_1 = 0\))
When port 1 is open, no current flows through the \(8\,\Omega\) resistor. Current \(I_2\) enters Port 2. The parallel branch across Port 2 is the shunt \(20\,\Omega\) in parallel with \((10 + 20 = 30\,\Omega)\): \[Z_{22} = \left.\frac{V_2}{I_2}\right|_{I_1 = 0} = 20 \parallel (10 + 20) = 12\,\Omega\] Voltage at Port 1 is the voltage across the first \(20\,\Omega\) resistor: \[V_1 = V_2 \times \frac{20}{10 + 20} = (12 I_2) \times \frac{2}{3} = 8 I_2 \implies Z_{12} = \left.\frac{V_1}{I_2}\right|_{I_1 = 0} = 8\,\Omega\]
Step 3: Checking Reciprocity & Symmetry
• Reciprocity: \(Z_{12} = Z_{21} = 8\,\Omega\). Thus, the network is RECIPROCAL.
• Symmetry: \(Z_{11} = 20\,\Omega \ne Z_{22} = 12\,\Omega\). Thus, the network is NOT SYMMETRICAL.
\[[Z] = \begin{bmatrix} 20 & 8 \\ 8 & 12 \end{bmatrix} \,\Omega\] Network is Reciprocal (\(Z_{12} = Z_{21}\)) but Asymmetrical (\(Z_{11} \ne Z_{22}\)).
Question 7 [5 Marks]: Features of Ideal Transformer & Core Flux Constancy

Features of Ideal Transformer:

  1. Zero winding resistance (\(R_1 = R_2 = 0\), zero copper losses).
  2. Infinite core permeability (\(\mu_r \to \infty\), zero magnetizing current required).
  3. Zero core losses (no hysteresis or eddy current losses, \(P_i = 0\)).
  4. Zero leakage flux (100% magnetic coupling, \(k = 1\), all flux is confined to core).

For the proof that core flux is independent of load current, see Derivations Sheet Section 2.

Question 8 [5 Marks]: DC Shunt Generator Generated EMF & Efficiency
DC Shunt Generator Numerical Chapter 8
Given:
Terminal voltage \(V_t = 200\text{ V}\)
Load current \(I_L = 96\text{ A}\)
Armature resistance \(R_a = 0.1\,\Omega\)
Shunt field resistance \(R_{sh} = 50\,\Omega\)
Iron and friction losses = \(2500\text{ W}\)
Required:
(a) Generated EMF \(E_g\)
(b) Commercial efficiency \(\eta\)
Principle & Formula:
\[I_{sh} = \frac{V_t}{R_{sh}}, \quad I_a = I_L + I_{sh}\] \[E_g = V_t + I_a R_a\] \[\eta = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} \times 100\% = \frac{V_t I_L}{V_t I_L + \text{Total Losses}} \times 100\%\]
Calculation:
1. Field current: \(I_{sh} = \frac{200}{50} = 4\text{ A}\).
2. Armature current: \(I_a = 96 + 4 = 100\text{ A}\).
3. Generated EMF: \[E_g = 200 + (100 \times 0.1) = 200 + 10 = 210\text{ V}\] 4. Power Output: \[P_{\text{out}} = V_t I_L = 200 \times 96 = 19,200\text{ W} = 19.2\text{ kW}\] 5. Copper Losses:
• Armature Cu loss: \(I_a^2 R_a = 100^2 \times 0.1 = 1000\text{ W}\).
• Shunt Cu loss: \(V_t I_{sh} = 200 \times 4 = 800\text{ W}\).
• Total Cu loss: \(1000 + 800 = 1800\text{ W}\).
6. Total Losses: \[\text{Total Losses} = \text{Cu Losses} + \text{Iron \& Friction} = 1800 + 2500 = 4300\text{ W}\] 7. Commercial Efficiency: \[\eta = \frac{19,200}{19,200 + 4,300} \times 100\% = \frac{19,200}{23,500} \times 100\% \approx 81.70\%\]
Final Results: (a) Generated EMF \(E_g = 210\text{ V}\), (b) Commercial Efficiency \(\eta = 81.70\%\).

Question 10 [9 Marks]: Short Notes (Series/Parallel Magnetic, Universal Motor, Starter, VR)

a) Series and Parallel Magnetic Circuits: In series magnetic circuits, the same flux \(\Phi\) passes through all sections; total \(\text{MMF} = \Phi(\mathcal{R}_1 + \mathcal{R}_2 + \mathcal{R}_g)\). In parallel circuits, total flux divides across branches: \(\Phi = \Phi_1 + \Phi_2\), and MMF across parallel paths is identical.

b) Universal Motor: A series-wound commutator motor designed to operate efficiently on either DC or single-phase AC. Operates at very high speeds (\(3000 - 15000\text{ rpm}\)), ideal for vacuum cleaners, blenders, and portable drills.

c) DC Motor Starter (3-Point Starter): Inserts starting resistors in series with armature to limit huge inrush currents (\(I_a = V/R_a\)). Incorporates a No-Volt Coil (NVC) to release the handle on power outage and an Overload Release (OLR) coil to trip on excessive load.

d) Voltage Regulation of Transformer: Defined as \(\%VR = \frac{V_{2,\text{no-load}} - V_{2,\text{full-load}}}{V_{2,\text{no-load}}} \times 100\). Measures the voltage drop due to internal resistance and leakage reactance.

Question 2 [1+4 Marks]: Inductor Switching & Derivatives at \(t = 0^+\)

Question Statement: Explain the behavior of an inductor when subjected to switching. In the network, switch is opened at \(t = 0\). Calculate \(v, \frac{dv}{dt}, \frac{d^2v}{dt^2}\) at \(t = 0^+\) if \(I = 2\text{A}, R = 200\,\Omega, L = 1\text{H}\). Given that \(v\) is the voltage across the inductor.

Higher Derivatives Calculation Chapter 1
Calculations:
Before switching: Switch closed, inductor in steady state carries \(i_L(0^-) = 2\text{A}\).
At \(t = 0^+\): Switch opens. Current source \(I = 2\text{A}\) must now split between \(R\) and \(L\).
Since \(i_L(0^+) = 2\text{A}\), all current from the source enters the inductor, meaning resistor current \(i_R(0^+) = I - i_L(0^+) = 2 - 2 = 0\text{ A}\).
Therefore, inductor voltage \(v(0^+) = v_R(0^+) = i_R(0^+) \times R = 0 \times 200 = 0\text{ V}\).
For \(t > 0\): \(i_R + i_L = I \implies \frac{v}{R} + i_L = I\).
Differentiate with respect to \(t\): \[\frac{1}{R}\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{di_L}{dt} = 0 \implies \frac{1}{R}\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{v}{L} = 0 \implies \frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{R}{L} v\] At \(t = 0^+\), since \(v(0^+) = 0\): \[\frac{dv}{dt}(0^+) = -\frac{200}{1}(0) = 0\text{ V/s}\] Differentiating again: \[\frac{d^2v}{dt^2} = -\frac{R}{L}\frac{dv}{dt} \implies \frac{d^2v}{dt^2}(0^+) = 0\text{ V/s}^2\]
Final Values: \(v(0^+) = 0\text{ V}\), \(\frac{dv}{dt}(0^+) = 0\text{ V/s}\), \(\frac{d^2v}{dt^2}(0^+) = 0\text{ V/s}^2\).

Question 8 [5 Marks]: Magnetic Circuit Air Gap MMF Numerical
Composite Magnetic Circuit MMF Chapter 6
Given:
Mean length \(l_m = 0.5\text{ m}\)
Cross-sectional area \(A = 100\text{ cm}^2 = 100 \times 10^{-4} = 0.01\text{ m}^2\)

Calculations:
Flux density: \[B = \frac{\Phi}{A} = \frac{0.002}{0.01} = 0.2\text{ Tesla}\] Length of iron path: \[l_i = l_m - l_g = 0.5 - 0.004 = 0.496\text{ m}\] Magnetizing force in iron: \[H_i = \frac{B}{\mu_0 \mu_r} = \frac{0.2}{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 800} \approx 198.94\text{ AT/m}\] MMF for iron path: \[\text{MMF}_i = H_i \times l_i = 198.94 \times 0.496 \approx 98.68\text{ AT}\] Magnetizing force in air gap: \[H_g = \frac{B}{\mu_0} = \frac{0.2}{4\pi \times 10^{-7}} \approx 159,155\text{ AT/m}\] MMF for air gap: \[\text{MMF}_g = H_g \times l_g = 159,155 \times 0.004 \approx 636.62\text{ AT}\] Total MMF required: \[\text{Total MMF} = \text{MMF}_i + \text{MMF}_g = 98.68 + 636.62 = 735.30\text{ AT}\] Exciting current: \(I = \frac{\text{MMF}}{N} = \frac{735.3}{200} \approx 3.68\text{ A}\).

Question 3 [6 Marks]: Series RLC Step Response (Classical Method)

Question Statement: A DC source of 100 V is suddenly applied at time \(t = 0\) to a series RLC circuit comprising \(R = 2\,\Omega, L = 0.5\text{H}\) and \(C = 1\text{F}\). Obtain the expression for current in the circuit by using classical method.

Series RLC Classical Solution Chapter 2
Calculations:
Characteristic equation: \[L s^2 + R s + \frac{1}{C} = 0 \implies 0.5 s^2 + 2 s + 1 = 0 \implies s^2 + 4s + 2 = 0\] Roots: \[s_{1,2} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 8}}{2} = -2 \pm \sqrt{2}\] Since roots are real, negative, and unequal (\(s_1 = -2 + \sqrt{2} \approx -0.586\), \(s_2 = -2 - \sqrt{2} \approx -3.414\)), the circuit is overdamped! \[i(t) = A_1 e^{s_1 t} + A_2 e^{s_2 t}\] Initial conditions:
1. \(i(0^+) = 0 \implies A_1 + A_2 = 0 \implies A_2 = -A_1\).
2. Across inductor at \(t = 0^+\): \(L \frac{di}{dt}(0^+) = V \implies \frac{di}{dt}(0^+) = \frac{100}{0.5} = 200\text{ A/s}\). \[\frac{di}{dt}(0^+) = s_1 A_1 + s_2 A_2 = A_1(s_1 - s_2) = A_1[(-2+\sqrt{2}) - (-2-\sqrt{2})] = A_1(2\sqrt{2}) = 200\] \[A_1 = \frac{200}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{100}{\sqrt{2}} = 50\sqrt{2} \approx 70.71\text{ A}\]
Final Expression: \[i(t) = 50\sqrt{2} \left[ e^{(-2+\sqrt{2})t} - e^{(-2-\sqrt{2})t} \right]\text{ A} \quad (t \ge 0)\]
