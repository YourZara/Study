Ch 5. Two-Port Parameters

8 hours · 8 marks

Two-port network theory describes linear networks with one input port and one output port using parameter matrices. Exam focus: definitions of Z, Y, ABCD, h, g; conversions (especially \(h\) from \(Z\), \(Z\) from ABCD); cascade via ABCD multiplication; reciprocity and symmetry tests.

5.1 Definitions

A two-port has four terminals grouped as port 1 (\(V_1,I_1\)) and port 2 (\(V_2,I_2\)). Standard sign convention: both currents \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) enter the network through the \(+\) terminals (except for transmission/ABCD parameters, where \(I_2\) is usually defined leaving port 2 — see below).

Any two of \(\{V_1,I_1,V_2,I_2\}\) may be chosen as independent; the other two are dependent. Each choice yields a different parameter set.

5.2 Parameter Sets

5.2.1 Z-parameters (open-circuit impedance)

\[ \begin{aligned} V_1 &= Z_{11}I_1 + Z_{12}I_2\\ V_2 &= Z_{21}I_1 + Z_{22}I_2 \end{aligned} \qquad \begin{bmatrix}V_1\\V_2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}Z_{11}&Z_{12}\\Z_{21}&Z_{22}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}I_1\\I_2\end{bmatrix} \]
ParameterDefinitionCondition
\(Z_{11}\)\(V_1/I_1\)\(I_2=0\) (port 2 open)
\(Z_{12}\)\(V_1/I_2\)\(I_1=0\)
\(Z_{21}\)\(V_2/I_1\)\(I_2=0\)
\(Z_{22}\)\(V_2/I_2\)\(I_1=0\)

\(Z_{11},Z_{22}\): driving-point impedances; \(Z_{12},Z_{21}\): transfer impedances.

5.2.2 Y-parameters (short-circuit admittance)

\[ \begin{aligned} I_1 &= Y_{11}V_1 + Y_{12}V_2\\ I_2 &= Y_{21}V_1 + Y_{22}V_2 \end{aligned} \]

Measured with the other port shorted (\(V=0\)). \(\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\) when both exist.

5.2.3 ABCD parameters (transmission / T-parameters)

Relate sending-end quantities to receiving-end quantities. Convention: \(I_2\) leaves port 2 (so \(I_2'=-I_2\) in Z-convention):

\[ \begin{aligned} V_1 &= A V_2 + B (-I_2)\\ I_1 &= C V_2 + D (-I_2) \end{aligned} \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \begin{bmatrix}V_1\\I_1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}V_2\\-I_2\end{bmatrix} \]
ParamMeaningCondition
\(A=V_1/V_2\)open-circuit voltage ratio\(I_2=0\)
\(B=V_1/(-I_2)\)short-circuit transfer impedance\(V_2=0\)
\(C=I_1/V_2\)open-circuit transfer admittance\(I_2=0\)
\(D=I_1/(-I_2)\)short-circuit current ratio\(V_2=0\)

5.2.4 A′B′C′D′ (inverse transmission)

\[ \begin{bmatrix}V_2\\-I_2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}A'&B'\\C'&D'\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}V_1\\I_1\end{bmatrix} \]

Useful when cascading is described from load toward source. Inverse of the ABCD matrix when \(\Delta_T=AD-BC\neq 0\).

5.2.5 h-parameters (hybrid)

\[ \begin{aligned} V_1 &= h_{11}I_1 + h_{12}V_2\\ I_2 &= h_{21}I_1 + h_{22}V_2 \end{aligned} \]
ParamNameCondition
\(h_{11}=V_1/I_1\)input impedance\(V_2=0\) (port 2 short)
\(h_{12}=V_1/V_2\)reverse voltage gain\(I_1=0\)
\(h_{21}=I_2/I_1\)forward current gain\(V_2=0\)
\(h_{22}=I_2/V_2\)output admittance\(I_1=0\)

Widely used for BJT small-signal models (\(h_{ie},h_{re},h_{fe},h_{oe}\)).

5.2.6 g-parameters (inverse hybrid)

\[ \begin{aligned} I_1 &= g_{11}V_1 + g_{12}I_2\\ V_2 &= g_{21}V_1 + g_{22}I_2 \end{aligned} \]

\(\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{h}^{-1}\) when both exist. Useful for some FET / dual formulations.

5.3 Relationships Between Parameter Sets

h in terms of Z

\[ h_{11}=\frac{\Delta_Z}{Z_{22}},\quad h_{12}=\frac{Z_{12}}{Z_{22}},\quad h_{21}=-\frac{Z_{21}}{Z_{22}},\quad h_{22}=\frac{1}{Z_{22}} \] where \(\Delta_Z=Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}\).

Z in terms of ABCD

\[ Z_{11}=\frac{A}{C},\quad Z_{12}=\frac{AD-BC}{C},\quad Z_{21}=\frac{1}{C},\quad Z_{22}=\frac{D}{C} \] (for reciprocal networks \(AD-BC=1\), so \(Z_{12}=Z_{21}=1/C\)).

Other useful conversions (summary)

From → ToKey relations
Z → Y\(\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\); \(Y_{11}=Z_{22}/\Delta_Z\), etc.
Z → ABCD\(A=Z_{11}/Z_{21}\), \(B=\Delta_Z/Z_{21}\), \(C=1/Z_{21}\), \(D=Z_{22}/Z_{21}\)
Y → ABCD\(A=-Y_{22}/Y_{21}\), \(B=-1/Y_{21}\), \(C=-\Delta_Y/Y_{21}\), \(D=-Y_{11}/Y_{21}\)
ABCD → h\(h_{11}=B/D\), \(h_{12}=(AD-BC)/D\), \(h_{21}=-1/D\), \(h_{22}=C/D\)

5.4 Interconnection of Two-Ports

ConnectionRuleWhen valid
Series (ports in series)\(\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{Z}_a+\mathbf{Z}_b\)loop currents compatible; no forbidden circulating paths
Parallel\(\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{Y}_a+\mathbf{Y}_b\)port voltages shared; Brune tests satisfied
Cascade (chain)\(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{T}_1\mathbf{T}_2\)port 2 of first feeds port 1 of second
Series–paralleladd \(\mathbf{h}\)hybrid interconnection
Parallel–seriesadd \(\mathbf{g}\)inverse hybrid interconnection
Cascade exam tip: convert each stage to ABCD, multiply left-to-right in signal-flow order, then convert back to Z/Y/h if required.

5.5 Reciprocity and Symmetry

Reciprocity

A network of bilateral passive elements (R, L, C, mutual transformers without dependent sources / gyrators) is reciprocal:

Parameter setReciprocity condition
Z\(Z_{12}=Z_{21}\)
Y\(Y_{12}=Y_{21}\)
ABCD\(AD-BC=1\)
h\(h_{12}=-h_{21}\)
g\(g_{12}=-g_{21}\)

Dependent sources generally destroy reciprocity.

Symmetry

A symmetric two-port looks the same if ports are swapped:

  • Z: \(Z_{11}=Z_{22}\) (and usually also reciprocal ⇒ \(Z_{12}=Z_{21}\))
  • Y: \(Y_{11}=Y_{22}\)
  • ABCD: \(A=D\)
  • h: \(h_{11}h_{22}-h_{12}h_{21}=1\) together with reciprocity, or check \(Z_{11}=Z_{22}\)

Solved Examples

Example 1 — Z-parameters of a T-network

Ladder / T

T-network: series arms \(Z_a\) (port 1 side), \(Z_b\) (port 2 side), shunt arm \(Z_c\) from junction to common return.

Solution. With \(I_2=0\): \(Z_{11}=Z_a+Z_c\), \(Z_{21}=Z_c\).

With \(I_1=0\): \(Z_{22}=Z_b+Z_c\), \(Z_{12}=Z_c\).

\[ \mathbf{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}Z_a+Z_c & Z_c\\ Z_c & Z_b+Z_c\end{bmatrix} \]

Reciprocal: \(Z_{12}=Z_{21}=Z_c\) always for this passive T.

Symmetric: iff \(Z_a=Z_b\) (then \(Z_{11}=Z_{22}\)).

Example 2 — Numerical T-network

Series arms \(2\,\Omega\) and \(3\,\Omega\), shunt \(6\,\Omega\). Find Z-parameters; check reciprocal/symmetric.

\[ Z_{11}=2+6=8,\quad Z_{22}=3+6=9,\quad Z_{12}=Z_{21}=6 \] \[ \mathbf{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}8&6\\6&9\end{bmatrix}\,\Omega \] Reciprocal yes (\(6=6\)); symmetric no (\(8\neq 9\)).

Example 3 — Ladder (two L-sections) via cascade

ABCD multiply

Cascade: series \(Z_1\) then shunt \(Y_2=1/Z_2\).

Series impedance alone:

\[ \mathbf{T}_{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}1&Z_1\\0&1\end{bmatrix} \]

Shunt admittance alone:

\[ \mathbf{T}_{Y}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\Y_2&1\end{bmatrix} \] \[ \mathbf{T}=\mathbf{T}_{Z}\mathbf{T}_{Y}=\begin{bmatrix}1+Z_1 Y_2 & Z_1\\ Y_2 & 1\end{bmatrix} \]

Then \(Z_{11}=A/C=(1+Z_1 Y_2)/Y_2=Z_2+Z_1\), \(Z_{21}=1/C=Z_2\), etc.

Example 4 — Cascade of two T-equivalent stages

Network A: \(A_1=2,\,B_1=3\,\Omega,\,C_1=1\,\text{S},\,D_1=2\). Network B: \(A_2=1,\,B_2=1\,\Omega,\,C_2=1\,\text{S},\,D_2=2\). Find overall ABCD and check reciprocity.

\[ \mathbf{T}=\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\1&2\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}5&8\\3&5\end{bmatrix} \]

\(\Delta=AD-BC=25-24=1\) ⇒ overall reciprocal (product of reciprocal networks).

\(A=5\neq D=5\) wait — here \(A=D=5\) so also symmetric overall. (Check: \(5\cdot5-8\cdot3=1\).)

Example 5 — Network with a dependent source

Dependent source

Port 1: voltage \(V_1\) across input. Series resistance \(R\) then a VCVS \(V_x=\mu V_1\) feeding port 2 through the controlled source (simplified unilateral amp model: \(I_2\) depends on \(V_1\)).

Classic resistive network: left series \(1\,\Omega\), controlled current source \(2I_1\) in parallel with \(2\,\Omega\) at port 2.

Find Y or h; test reciprocity.

With dependent sources, compute parameters from definitions carefully.

Illustration with Z for a network where \(V_2=k I_1\) and input is resistive: often \(Z_{21}\neq Z_{12}\).

Conclusion pattern for exams: if \(Z_{12}\neq Z_{21}\) (or \(AD-BC\neq 1\)), the network is not reciprocal — expected when controlled sources are present.

Example 6 — h from Z

Given \(\mathbf{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}8&6\\6&9\end{bmatrix}\). Find \(\mathbf{h}\).

\(\Delta_Z=72-36=36\). \[ h_{11}=\frac{36}{9}=4,\quad h_{12}=\frac{6}{9}=\frac{2}{3},\quad h_{21}=-\frac{6}{9}=-\frac{2}{3},\quad h_{22}=\frac{1}{9} \] Check reciprocity: \(h_{12}=-h_{21}\) ✓.

Example 7 — Z from ABCD

\(A=5,\,B=8,\,C=3,\,D=5\). Find Z-parameters.

\[ Z_{11}=\frac{A}{C}=\frac{5}{3},\quad Z_{21}=\frac{1}{C}=\frac{1}{3},\quad Z_{22}=\frac{D}{C}=\frac{5}{3},\quad Z_{12}=\frac{AD-BC}{C}=\frac{1}{3} \] Symmetric and reciprocal as expected.

Most Probable Questions

Q1. Define Z-parameters of a two-port network. How are they determined? 5 marks
Open-circuit impedance parameters relating \(V_1,V_2\) to \(I_1,I_2\). Each \(Z_{ij}\) is found by driving one port with current and opening the other (\(I=0\)), then taking the appropriate \(V/I\) ratio (see §5.2.1).
Q2. Define Y, ABCD, h and g parameters briefly. 8 marks
Y: short-circuit admittances (\(I\) vs \(V\)). ABCD: transmission matrix mapping \((V_2,-I_2)\) to \((V_1,I_1)\). h: hybrid (\(V_1,I_2\) vs \(I_1,V_2\)). g: inverse hybrid (\(I_1,V_2\) vs \(V_1,I_2\)). Give defining equations from §5.2.
Q3. Express h-parameters in terms of Z-parameters. 5 marks
\(h_{11}=\Delta_Z/Z_{22}\), \(h_{12}=Z_{12}/Z_{22}\), \(h_{21}=-Z_{21}/Z_{22}\), \(h_{22}=1/Z_{22}\) with \(\Delta_Z=Z_{11}Z_{22}-Z_{12}Z_{21}\).
Q4. Express Z-parameters in terms of ABCD parameters. 4 marks
\(Z_{11}=A/C\), \(Z_{12}=(AD-BC)/C\), \(Z_{21}=1/C\), \(Z_{22}=D/C\).
Q5. Find Z-parameters of a T-network with series arms \(Z_a,Z_b\) and shunt \(Z_c\). State reciprocity/symmetry conditions. 7 marks
See Example 1: \(\mathbf{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}Z_a+Z_c&Z_c\\Z_c&Z_b+Z_c\end{bmatrix}\). Always reciprocal; symmetric iff \(Z_a=Z_b\).
Q6. Explain series, parallel and cascade interconnection of two-ports. 6 marks
Series ⇒ add Z-matrices; parallel ⇒ add Y-matrices; cascade ⇒ multiply ABCD (T) matrices in order. Mention validity constraints (Brune tests) briefly.
Q7. State reciprocity and symmetry conditions for Z and ABCD parameters. 5 marks
Reciprocal: \(Z_{12}=Z_{21}\) or \(AD-BC=1\). Symmetric: \(Z_{11}=Z_{22}\) or \(A=D\). Passive RLC networks without dependent sources are reciprocal.
Q8. Two networks in cascade have ABCD matrices \(\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\) and \(\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\). Find overall ABCD. 6 marks
Product \(\begin{bmatrix}5&8\\3&5\end{bmatrix}\) (Example 4). \(\Delta=1\) ⇒ reciprocal; \(A=D\) ⇒ symmetric.
Q9. Why do networks with dependent sources often fail reciprocity? 3 marks
Controlled sources introduce unilateral coupling: excitation at one port may affect the other differently than the reverse experiment, so \(Z_{12}\neq Z_{21}\) (or \(AD-BC\neq 1\)).
Q10. For \(\mathbf{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}8&6\\6&9\end{bmatrix}\Omega\), find h-parameters. 5 marks
Example 6: \(h_{11}=4\,\Omega\), \(h_{12}=2/3\), \(h_{21}=-2/3\), \(h_{22}=1/9\,\text{S}\).
