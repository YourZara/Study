Ch 4. Network Transfer Function and Frequency Response

8 hours · 8 marks

This chapter links Laplace-domain network functions to steady-state sinusoidal behaviour. Exam questions typically ask definitions (network function, poles/zeros), Bode asymptotic construction for given \(H(s)\), bandwidth/Q concepts, and filter classification.

4.1 Complex Frequency

A general exponential signal may be written \(e^{st}\) where the complex frequency is

\[ s = \sigma + j\omega \]
  • \(\sigma\) (nepers/s) — damping / growth rate of the envelope
  • \(\omega\) (rad/s) — angular frequency of oscillation

Special cases:

\(s\)SignalInterpretation
\(s = 0\)DC / constantsteady value
\(s = \sigma\) (real)\(e^{\sigma t}\)pure exponential (no oscillation)
\(s = j\omega\)\(e^{j\omega t}\)steady-state sinusoid (phasor analysis)
\(s = \sigma + j\omega\)\(e^{\sigma t}e^{j\omega t}\)damped / growing sinusoid

Replacing \(s\) by \(j\omega\) in a network function yields the sinusoidal steady-state frequency response.

4.2 Transfer Functions of Two-Port Networks

Network function (definition)

A network function (driving-point or transfer) is the Laplace-domain ratio of a response transform to an excitation transform, with zero initial conditions:

\[ H(s) = \frac{\text{Response}(s)}{\text{Excitation}(s)} \]

Common transfer functions of a two-port:

NameDefinitionCondition
Voltage transfer\(G_v(s) = V_2(s)/V_1(s)\)port 2 open or specified load
Current transfer\(\alpha(s) = I_2(s)/I_1(s)\)port 2 short or specified
Transfer impedance\(Z_{21}(s) = V_2(s)/I_1(s)\)\(I_2=0\)
Transfer admittance\(Y_{21}(s) = I_2(s)/V_1(s)\)\(V_2=0\)
Driving-point impedance\(Z_{\text{in}}(s) = V_1(s)/I_1(s)\)same port

In general a rational network function has the form

\[ H(s) = K\,\frac{(s-z_1)(s-z_2)\cdots(s-z_m)}{(s-p_1)(s-p_2)\cdots(s-p_n)} \] where \(K\) is a gain constant, \(z_i\) are zeros and \(p_k\) are poles.

4.3 Poles and Zeros

  • Zeros: values of \(s\) that make \(H(s)=0\) (numerator roots). At a zero the response vanishes for that complex-frequency excitation.
  • Poles: values of \(s\) that make \(H(s)\to\infty\) (denominator roots). Poles determine natural modes and stability.
  • Pole-zero diagram: zeros marked ○, poles marked × in the \(s\)-plane.
Stability (LTI lumped networks): all poles must lie in the open left-half plane (or simple poles on the \(j\omega\) axis for lossless LC). Right-half-plane poles ⇒ unstable.

Example — locate poles and zeros. Given

\[ H(s)=\frac{10(s^2+8s+15)}{s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)} \]

Numerator: \(s^2+8s+15=(s+3)(s+5)\) ⇒ zeros at \(s=-3,-5\).

Denominator: \(s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)=0\) ⇒ poles at \(s=0\) (double), \(s=-3\), \(s=-20\).

Note the zero at \(-3\) cancels one factor of \((s+3)\) if allowed, leaving an effective pole-zero set after cancellation (check before Bode drawing).

4.4 Magnitude and Phase Response

On the \(j\omega\) axis, \(H(j\omega)=|H(j\omega)|\,e^{j\phi(\omega)}\):

\[ |H(j\omega)| = \text{magnitude},\qquad \phi(\omega)=\angle H(j\omega)=\text{phase} \]

In decibels:

\[ |H|_{\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}|H(j\omega)| \]

Useful identities for Bode construction:

\[ 20\log|AB|=20\log|A|+20\log|B|,\qquad 20\log\Bigl|\frac{A}{B}\Bigr|=20\log|A|-20\log|B| \] \[ \angle(AB)=\angle A+\angle B,\qquad \angle(A/B)=\angle A-\angle B \]

4.5 Bode Diagrams

A Bode diagram plots \(20\log|H|\) (dB) and \(\phi\) (degrees) vs \(\omega\) on a log frequency axis. Asymptotic Bode plots use straight-line approximations that are fast and exam-friendly.

Standard factors

Write \(H(s)\) in Bode (corner-frequency) form before plotting:

\[ H(s)=K\,\frac{\prod(1+s/\omega_{z})}{\prod(1+s/\omega_{p})\cdot\prod\bigl(1+2\zeta s/\omega_n + s^2/\omega_n^2\bigr)} \] (also include \(s^{\pm r}\) factors for poles/zeros at the origin).

Asymptotic magnitude rules

FactorAsymptote
Constant \(K\)\(20\log|K|\) dB horizontal
\(s\) (zero at origin)\(+20\) dB/decade through \(0\) dB at \(\omega=1\)
\(1/s\) (pole at origin)\(-20\) dB/decade through \(0\) dB at \(\omega=1\)
\(1+s/\omega_c\) (real zero)\(0\) dB for \(\omega\ll\omega_c\); \(+20\) dB/dec for \(\omega\gg\omega_c\); corner at \(\omega_c\)
\(1/(1+s/\omega_c)\) (real pole)\(0\) dB then \(-20\) dB/dec after \(\omega_c\)
Quadratic \(\omega_n,\zeta\)\(0\) then \(\pm 40\) dB/dec after \(\omega_n\); peaking depends on \(\zeta\)

Asymptotic phase rules (real poles/zeros)

  • Constant \(K>0\): \(0^\circ\); \(K<0\): \(\pm 180^\circ\)
  • Pole at origin \(1/s\): \(-90^\circ\) constant; zero at origin \(s\): \(+90^\circ\)
  • Real pole at \(\omega_c\): phase goes \(0^\circ\to -90^\circ\); asymptotic sketch often uses \(0^\circ\) until \(0.1\omega_c\), linear to \(-90^\circ\) at \(10\omega_c\)
  • Real zero at \(\omega_c\): \(0^\circ\to +90^\circ\) over the same decade span
  • Quadratic: \(0^\circ\to \pm 180^\circ\) centred at \(\omega_n\)

4.6 Bandwidth, High-Q and Low-Q Circuits

For a second-order band-pass or resonant circuit with centre (resonant) frequency \(\omega_0\) and half-power frequencies \(\omega_1,\omega_2\):

\[ \text{BW}=\omega_2-\omega_1=\frac{R}{L}\quad\text{(series RLC)},\qquad Q=\frac{\omega_0}{\text{BW}} \]
  • High-Q (\(Q\gg 1\), typically \(Q>10\)): sharp resonance, narrow bandwidth, lightly damped; magnitude peak near \(\omega_0\).
  • Low-Q (\(Q\) small, e.g. \(Q<0.5\)): overdamped / broad response; poles are real or widely spaced; little peaking.

For the standard quadratic factor \(s^2+2\zeta\omega_n s+\omega_n^2\), \(Q=1/(2\zeta)\). High-Q ⇔ small damping ratio \(\zeta\).

4.7 Filters: HP, LP, Band-Stop, Band-Pass

TypePass / stop behaviourTypical first-order TF (RC)
Low-pass (LP)passes low \(\omega\), attenuates high\(\dfrac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}\)
High-pass (HP)passes high \(\omega\), attenuates low\(\dfrac{s}{s+\omega_c}\)
Band-pass (BP)passes a band around \(\omega_0\)\(\dfrac{(\text{BW})s}{s^2+(\text{BW})s+\omega_0^2}\)
Band-stop / notchrejects a band around \(\omega_0\)\(\dfrac{s^2+\omega_0^2}{s^2+(\text{BW})s+\omega_0^2}\)

Order equals degree of denominator (after proper reduction). Cascading stages raises order and steepens roll-off (\(\pm 20n\) dB/decade for order \(n\) real poles).

Solved Examples — Asymptotic Bode Plots

Example 1

Exam type

Sketch the asymptotic magnitude Bode plot of

\[ H(s)=\frac{10(s^2+8s+15)}{s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)} \]

Solution. Factor and rewrite in corner form.

\[ s^2+8s+15=(s+3)(s+5) \] \[ H(s)=\frac{10(s+3)(s+5)}{s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)} \]

Cancel \((s+3)\) (assuming no hidden RHP issues — here both cancel safely for magnitude asymptotes):

\[ H(s)=\frac{10(s+5)}{s^2(0.05s+1)}=\frac{10\cdot 5\,(1+s/5)}{s^2\cdot 1\cdot(1+s/20)}=\frac{50(1+s/5)}{s^2(1+s/20)} \]

Corners / factors:

  • \(K=50\) ⇒ \(20\log 50\approx 34\) dB
  • Double pole at origin: start with slope \(-40\) dB/dec
  • Zero at \(\omega=5\) rad/s: slope increases by \(+20\) → becomes \(-20\) dB/dec
  • Pole at \(\omega=20\) rad/s: slope decreases by \(20\) → back to \(-40\) dB/dec

To draw: pick a reference on the \(-40\) dB/dec line from the \(K/s^2\) behaviour (at \(\omega=1\), \(|H|\approx 50\) ⇒ \(34\) dB if only \(K/s^2\)), then break at \(5\) and \(20\).

Example 2

Exam type

\[ T(s)=\frac{100(s+5)}{s^2(s^2+21s+20)} \]

Solution. Factor \(s^2+21s+20=(s+1)(s+20)\).

\[ T(s)=\frac{100(s+5)}{s^2(s+1)(s+20)}=\frac{100\cdot 5\,(1+s/5)}{s^2\cdot 1\cdot 20\,(1+s)(1+s/20)}=\frac{25(1+s/5)}{s^2(1+s)(1+s/20)} \]

Corners: poles at \(0\) (double), \(1\), \(20\); zero at \(5\). Gain \(20\log 25\approx 28\) dB.

Slope sequence: start \(-40\) dB/dec → after \(\omega=1\): \(-60\) → after \(\omega=5\): \(-40\) → after \(\omega=20\): \(-60\) dB/dec.

Example 3

Exam type

\[ H(s)=\frac{100s(s+5)}{(s+1)(s^2+10s+100)} \]

Solution. Quadratic: \(s^2+10s+100\) ⇒ \(\omega_n=10\), \(2\zeta\omega_n=10\) ⇒ \(\zeta=0.5\) (moderately damped, \(Q=1\)).

\[ H(s)=\frac{100s\cdot 5(1+s/5)}{1\cdot(1+s)\cdot 100\bigl(1+s/10+s^2/100\bigr)}=\frac{5\,s\,(1+s/5)}{(1+s)\bigl(1+0.1s+0.01s^2\bigr)} \]

Factors: zero at origin (\(+20\) dB/dec start), real pole at \(1\), real zero at \(5\), complex poles at \(\omega_n=10\) (\(-40\) dB/dec after).

Slope: \(+20\) → after \(1\): \(0\) → after \(5\): \(+20\) → after \(10\): \(-20\) dB/dec.

Example 4

Exam type

\[ G(s)=\frac{500(s+2)}{s(s^2+15s+121)} \]

Solution. \(\omega_n=\sqrt{121}=11\), \(2\zeta\omega_n=15\) ⇒ \(\zeta=15/(22)\approx 0.682\) (low peaking).

\[ G(s)=\frac{500\cdot 2(1+s/2)}{s\cdot 121\bigl(1+15s/121+s^2/121\bigr)}=\frac{(1000/121)\,(1+s/2)}{s\,(1+\cdots)}\approx\frac{8.26\,(1+s/2)}{s(\cdots)} \]

Start: pole at origin (\(-20\) dB/dec); zero at \(2\) → slope \(0\); quadratic poles at \(11\) → slope \(-40\) dB/dec. \(20\log 8.26\approx 18.3\) dB sets the mid-band plateau level approximately.

Most Probable Questions

Q1. Define network function. Distinguish driving-point and transfer functions. 4 marks
A network function is the Laplace ratio of a response to an excitation with zero initial conditions. A driving-point function relates voltage and current at the same port (\(Z_{\text{in}},Y_{\text{in}}\)). A transfer function relates quantities at different ports or different variable types (\(V_2/V_1\), \(V_2/I_1\), etc.).
Q2. What are poles and zeros of a network function? How do they relate to stability? 5 marks
Zeros are roots of the numerator (\(H=0\)); poles are roots of the denominator (\(H\to\infty\)). For BIBO stability of a causal LTI network, all poles must have negative real parts (left-half plane); simple \(j\omega\)-axis poles are acceptable only in special lossless cases.
Q3. Explain complex frequency \(s=\sigma+j\omega\). When is \(s=j\omega\) used? 4 marks
\(s\) generalises exponential signals \(e^{st}\). \(\sigma\) sets growth/decay; \(\omega\) is oscillation rate. Setting \(s=j\omega\) recovers AC steady-state phasor analysis and frequency response \(H(j\omega)\).
Q4. Draw the asymptotic Bode magnitude plot of \(H(s)=10(s^2+8s+15)/[s^2(s+3)(0.05s+1)]\). 8 marks
Follow Example 1: cancel \((s+3)\), rewrite as \(50(1+s/5)/[s^2(1+s/20)]\). Slopes: \(-40\) → break at \(5\) to \(-20\) → break at \(20\) to \(-40\). Mark \(20\log 50\approx 34\) dB reference for the \(K/s^2\) asymptote.
Q5. State the rules for constructing asymptotic magnitude and phase Bode plots. 6 marks
Magnitude: constants contribute \(20\log|K|\); each origin pole/zero \(\mp 20\) dB/dec; each real corner changes slope by \(\pm 20\) dB/dec; quadratic by \(\pm 40\). Phase: origin pole/zero \(\mp 90^\circ\); each real factor contributes a \(0\to\mp 90^\circ\) transition spanning roughly \(0.1\omega_c\) to \(10\omega_c\); add contributions.
Q6. Define bandwidth and quality factor. Differentiate high-Q and low-Q circuits. 5 marks
BW is the width between half-power frequencies; \(Q=\omega_0/\text{BW}\). High-Q: narrow BW, sharp peak, small \(\zeta\). Low-Q: wide BW, heavy damping, little or no resonance peak.
Q7. Classify filters (LP, HP, BP, BS) with typical transfer functions. 6 marks
LP: \(\omega_c/(s+\omega_c)\); HP: \(s/(s+\omega_c)\); BP: \((\text{BW})s/(s^2+(\text{BW})s+\omega_0^2)\); BS: \((s^2+\omega_0^2)/(s^2+(\text{BW})s+\omega_0^2)\). Classify by which frequency bands are passed vs attenuated.
Q8. For \(T(s)=100(s+5)/[s^2(s^2+21s+20)]\), find corner frequencies and asymptotic slopes. 7 marks
See Example 2. Corners at \(1\), \(5\), \(20\) plus double pole at \(0\). Slopes: \(-40\to-60\to-40\to-60\) dB/dec.
Q9. Sketch Bode asymptotes for \(G(s)=500(s+2)/[s(s^2+15s+121)]\). 8 marks
Example 4: pole at \(0\), zero at \(2\), \(\omega_n=11\). Slopes \(-20\to 0\to-40\). Gain factor \(\approx 8.26\) ⇒ about \(18.3\) dB plateau.
Q10. What is the significance of \(\zeta\) (or \(Q\)) in a quadratic Bode factor? 3 marks
It controls peaking near \(\omega_n\). Small \(\zeta\) (high \(Q\)) → resonant peak above the asymptote; \(\zeta\approx 0.707\) → maximally flat; larger \(\zeta\) → no peak, smoother magnitude.
