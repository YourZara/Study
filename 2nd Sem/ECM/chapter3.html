Ch 3. Transient Analysis Using Laplace Transform

7 hours · 7 marks

3.1 Introduction

The Laplace transform converts linear constant-coefficient ODEs in time \(t\) into algebraic equations in complex frequency \(s\). Initial conditions enter automatically through differentiation theorems, so switched RLC problems become circuit algebra with impedances \(sL\), \(1/(sC)\) plus IC sources.

Definition

\[ F(s)=\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} f(t)e^{-st}\,dt,\qquad f(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\}. \] (IOE exams usually take the lower limit as \(0^-\) so impulses and ICs at switching are included.)

Laplace of common functions

\(f(t)\) for \(t\ge 0\)\(F(s)\)
\(u(t)\) (unit step)\(1/s\)
\(\delta(t)\)\(1\)
\(t\)\(1/s^2\)
\(e^{-at}\)\(1/(s+a)\)
\(t e^{-at}\)\(1/(s+a)^2\)
\(\sin\omega t\)\(\omega/(s^2+\omega^2)\)
\(\cos\omega t\)\(s/(s^2+\omega^2)\)
\(e^{-at}\sin\omega t\)\(\omega/((s+a)^2+\omega^2)\)
\(e^{-at}\cos\omega t\)\((s+a)/((s+a)^2+\omega^2)\)

Operational properties (exam essentials)

\[ \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\}=sF(s)-f(0^-),\qquad \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\}=s^2F(s)-sf(0^-)-f'(0^-), \] \[ \mathcal{L}\Bigl\{\int_{0^-}^{t}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\}=\frac{F(s)}{s},\qquad \mathcal{L}\{e^{-at}f(t)\}=F(s+a). \]

s-domain models of R, L, C (with ICs)

  • Resistor: \(V(s)=RI(s)\).
  • Inductor: \(V(s)=sL\,I(s)-L\,i(0^-)\)  (series voltage source \(L i(0^-)\) aiding the current direction), equivalently a parallel current source \(i(0^-)/s\) with impedance \(sL\).
  • Capacitor: \(V(s)=\dfrac{1}{sC}I(s)+\dfrac{v(0^-)}{s}\)  (series voltage source \(v(0^-)/s\)), equivalently parallel current source \(C v(0^-)\) with impedance \(1/(sC)\).

Procedure: (1) find \(i_L(0^-)\), \(v_C(0^-)\); (2) replace circuit by s-domain impedances + IC sources; (3) solve for \(I(s)\) or \(V(s)\) by nodal/mesh/Ohm; (4) partial-fraction expand; (5) inverse Laplace to \(t\)-domain for \(t>0\).

Partial fractions (quick patterns)

\[ \frac{N(s)}{(s+a)(s+b)}=\frac{A}{s+a}+\frac{B}{s+b},\quad A=\frac{N(-a)}{b-a},\ B=\frac{N(-b)}{a-b}. \] \[ \frac{N(s)}{(s+a)^2}=\frac{A}{s+a}+\frac{B}{(s+a)^2}. \] For quadratic underdamped factors, complete to \((s+\alpha)^2+\omega_d^2\) and match sine/cosine transforms.

3.2 Response of R-L and R-C Circuits

3.2.1 DC excitation

Series RL, \(v_s=V_s u(t)\), \(i(0^-)=I_0\):

\[ \bigl(R+sL\bigr)I(s)= \frac{V_s}{s}+L I_0 \Rightarrow I(s)=\frac{V_s/L}{s(s+R/L)}+\frac{I_0}{s+R/L}. \] Inverse (or recognize standard form):

\[ i(t)=\frac{V_s}{R}\bigl(1-e^{-(R/L)t}\bigr)+I_0 e^{-(R/L)t},\quad t>0. \]

Worked Example 1 — Switched RL (Laplace)

\(R=4\,\Omega\), \(L=2\,\text{H}\), \(V_s=12\,\text{V}\), \(i(0^-)=0\).

\[ I(s)=\frac{12/s}{4+2s}=\frac{12}{s(2s+4)}=\frac{6}{s(s+2)} =\frac{3}{s}-\frac{3}{s+2}. \] \[ i(t)=3\bigl(1-e^{-2t}\bigr)\,\text{A}. \]

Final answer: \(i(t)=3(1-e^{-2t})\,\text{A}\).

3.2.2 Exponential excitation

\(v_s=V_m e^{-\alpha t}u(t)\Rightarrow V_s(s)=V_m/(s+\alpha)\). Series RC with zero IC:

\[ I(s)=\frac{V_m/(s+\alpha)}{R+1/(sC)}=\frac{V_m\,sC}{(s+\alpha)(RCs+1)}, \] \[ V_C(s)=\frac{1}{sC}I(s)=\frac{V_m}{(s+\alpha)(s+1/(RC))}. \] Partial fractions → \(v_C(t)\) same as classical undetermined-coefficient result.

Worked Example 2 — RC with exponential source (Laplace)

\(R=2\,\Omega\), \(C=1\,\text{F}\), \(v_s=10e^{-2t}\), \(v_C(0^-)=0\). Voltage divider in the \(s\)-domain:

\[ V_C(s)=\frac{1/(sC)}{R+1/(sC)}\cdot\frac{10}{s+2} =\frac{1}{RCs+1}\cdot\frac{10}{s+2} =\frac{10}{(2s+1)(s+2)} =\frac{5}{(s+1/2)(s+2)}. \] Partial fractions \(V_C(s)=\dfrac{A}{s+1/2}+\dfrac{B}{s+2}\):

\[ A=\frac{5}{-1/2+2}=\frac{5}{3/2}=\frac{10}{3},\qquad B=\frac{5}{-2+1/2}=\frac{5}{-3/2}=-\frac{10}{3}. \] \[ v_C(t)=\frac{10}{3}\bigl(e^{-t/2}-e^{-2t}\bigr)\,\text{V}. \]

This matches the classical undetermined-coefficient result in Chapter 2.

Final answer: \(v_C(t)=\dfrac{10}{3}\bigl(e^{-t/2}-e^{-2t}\bigr)\,\text{V}\).

3.2.3 Sinusoidal excitation

\(v_s=V_m\cos\omega t\,u(t)\Rightarrow V_s(s)=V_m s/(s^2+\omega^2)\). For series RL, zero IC:

\[ I(s)=\frac{V_m s}{(s^2+\omega^2)(R+sL)}. \] Expand into terms \(As+B\) over \(s^2+\omega^2\) and \(C/(s+R/L)\); invert to transient exponential + steady AC.

Worked Example 3 — Switched RC to DC (Laplace)

\(R=5\,\Omega\), \(C=0.2\,\text{F}\), \(V_s=10\,\text{V}\), \(v_C(0^-)=0\). \(\tau=RC=1\,\text{s}\).

\[ V_C(s)=\frac{10/s}{5+1/(0.2s)}\cdot\frac{1}{0.2s} =\frac{10}{s(1+s)}=\frac{10}{s(s+1)} =\frac{10}{s}-\frac{10}{s+1}. \] (Using voltage divider \(V_C=\frac{1/(sC)}{R+1/(sC)}\cdot V_s/s=\frac{1}{RCs+1}\cdot 10/s\).)

\[ v_C(t)=10\bigl(1-e^{-t}\bigr)\,\text{V}. \]

Final answer: \(v_C(t)=10(1-e^{-t})\,\text{V}\).

3.3 Response of Series R-L-C Circuits

Series impedance \(Z(s)=R+sL+1/(sC)\). With source \(V_s(s)\) and ICs:

\[ I(s)=\frac{V_s(s)+L i(0^-)-v_C(0^-)/s}{R+sL+1/(sC)}. \] \[ V_C(s)=\frac{v_C(0^-)}{s}+\frac{1}{sC}I(s). \]

3.3.1 DC excitation

\(V_s(s)=V_0/s\), often zero IC \(\Rightarrow\)

\[ I(s)=\frac{V_0/s}{R+sL+1/(sC)}=\frac{V_0}{L}\cdot\frac{1}{s^2+(R/L)s+1/(LC)}. \] Complete the square / factor depending on damping; invert using tables.

Worked Example 4 — Series RLC step, underdamped (Laplace)

\(R=2\,\Omega\), \(L=1\,\text{H}\), \(C=1/5\,\text{F}\), \(V_0=10\,\text{V}\), zero IC. \(\alpha=R/(2L)=1\), \(\omega_0=\sqrt{5}\), \(\omega_d=2\).

\[ I(s)=\frac{10}{s^2+2s+5}=\frac{10}{(s+1)^2+4}. \] \[ i(t)=10\cdot\frac{1}{2}e^{-t}\sin 2t=5e^{-t}\sin 2t\,\text{A}. \] \[ V_C(s)=\frac{1}{sC}I(s)=\frac{5}{s}\cdot\frac{10}{(s+1)^2+4}=\frac{50}{s\bigl((s+1)^2+4\bigr)}. \] Partial fractions → \(v_C(t)=10+e^{-t}(A\cos 2t+B\sin 2t)\); with \(v_C(0)=0\), \(i(0)=0\): \(A=-10\), and from \(i=C v_C'\) get \(B\).

Standard result:

\[ v_C(t)=10-e^{-t}(10\cos 2t+5\sin 2t)\,\text{V}. \]

Final answer: \(i(t)=5e^{-t}\sin 2t\,\text{A}\); \(v_C(t)=10-e^{-t}(10\cos 2t+5\sin 2t)\,\text{V}\).

3.3.2 Exponential excitation

\(V_s(s)=V_m/(s+\alpha)\). Then

\[ I(s)=\frac{V_m/(s+\alpha)}{R+sL+1/(sC)} \] (zero IC). Factor denominator; expand including the \((s+\alpha)\) pole unless cancelled.

3.3.3 Sinusoidal excitation

\(V_s(s)=V_m s/(s^2+\omega^2)\) or \(\omega V_m/(s^2+\omega^2)\). \(I(s)\) is a rational function; invert to damped natural terms + undamped steady sinusoid (forced response).

Worked Example 5 — Series RLC with nonzero IC

\(R=3\,\Omega\), \(L=1\,\text{H}\), \(C=1/2\,\text{F}\), source shorted for \(t>0\) (source-free), \(i(0^-)=2\,\text{A}\), \(v_C(0^-)=4\,\text{V}\).

\[ I(s)=\frac{L i(0^-)-v_C(0^-)/s}{R+sL+1/(sC)} =\frac{2-4/s}{3+s+2/s} =\frac{(2s-4)/s}{(s^2+3s+2)/s} =\frac{2(s-2)}{(s+1)(s+2)}. \] Partial fractions \(I(s)=\dfrac{A}{s+1}+\dfrac{B}{s+2}\):

\[ A=\frac{2(-1-2)}{-1+2}=-6,\qquad B=\frac{2(-2-2)}{-2+1}=8. \] \[ i(t)=\bigl(-6e^{-t}+8e^{-2t}\bigr)\,\text{A}. \] Check: \(i(0)=-6+8=2\,\text{A}\) matches \(i(0^-)\).

Final answer: \(i(t)=-6e^{-t}+8e^{-2t}\,\text{A}\).

3.4 Response of Parallel R-L-C Circuits

Parallel admittance \(Y(s)=1/R + 1/(sL) + sC\). With current source \(I_s(s)\) and ICs (using parallel IC current sources \(C v(0^-)\) out of the positive node and \(i_L(0^-)/s\) into/out per sign convention):

\[ V(s)=\frac{I_s(s)+C v(0^-)-i_L(0^-)/s}{sC+1/R+1/(sL)}. \]

3.4.1 DC excitation

\(i_s=I_0 u(t)\Rightarrow I_s=I_0/s\). For zero IC:

\[ V(s)=\frac{I_0/s}{sC+G+1/(sL)}=\frac{I_0}{C}\cdot\frac{1}{s^2+(G/C)s+1/(LC)},\quad G=1/R. \] Invert according to damping (\(\alpha=G/(2C)=1/(2RC)\)).

Worked Example 6 — Parallel RLC, DC current (Laplace)

\(R=1\,\Omega\), \(L=1\,\text{H}\), \(C=1\,\text{F}\), \(I_0=2\,\text{A}\), zero IC. \(\alpha=0.5\), \(\omega_d=\sqrt{3}/2\).

\[ V(s)=\frac{2}{s^2+s+1}=\frac{2}{(s+1/2)^2+3/4}. \] \[ v(t)=2\cdot\frac{1}{\omega_d}e^{-t/2}\sin\omega_d t =\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}e^{-t/2}\sin\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\Bigr)\,\text{V}. \]

Final answer: \(v(t)=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\,e^{-t/2}\sin\bigl(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\bigr)\,\text{V}\) (matches Ch2 classical Example 4).

3.4.2 Exponential excitation

\(i_s=I_m e^{-\alpha t}\Rightarrow I_s(s)=I_m/(s+\alpha)\).

\[ V(s)=\frac{I_m/(s+\alpha)}{sC+G+1/(sL)}. \] Clear the denominator; partial-fraction including pole at \(-\alpha\); invert.

Worked Example 7 — Parallel RLC exponential current

Same \(R=L=C=1\), \(i_s=3e^{-2t}u(t)\), zero IC.

\[ V(s)=\frac{3/(s+2)}{s+1+1/s} =\frac{3s}{(s+2)(s^2+s+1)}. \] Expand \(V(s)=\dfrac{A}{s+2}+\dfrac{Bs+C}{s^2+s+1}\). Cover-up at \(s=-2\):

\[ A=\frac{3(-2)}{(-2)^2+(-2)+1}=\frac{-6}{3}=-2. \] Clear denominators: \(A(s^2+s+1)+(Bs+C)(s+2)=3s\). With \(A=-2\):

\[ -2s^2-2s-2 + Bs(s+2)+C(s+2)=3s \Rightarrow (-2+B)s^2+( -2+2B+C)s+(-2+2C)=3s. \] Equate: \(B=2\), \(-2+2C=0\Rightarrow C=1\), and check linear: \(-2+4+1=3\) ✓. So

\[ V(s)=\frac{-2}{s+2}+\frac{2s+1}{(s+1/2)^2+(\sqrt{3}/2)^2}. \] Rewrite \(2s+1=2(s+1/2)+0\):

\[ v(t)=-2e^{-2t}+2e^{-t/2}\cos\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\Bigr)\,\text{V}. \]

Final answer: \(v(t)=-2e^{-2t}+2e^{-t/2}\cos\bigl(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\bigr)\,\text{V}\).

Most Probable Questions

Q1. Define the Laplace transform. Tabulate transforms of \(u(t)\), \(e^{-at}\), \(\sin\omega t\), \(\cos\omega t\). 4 marks
\(F(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-st}dt\). Values: \(1/s\), \(1/(s+a)\), \(\omega/(s^2+\omega^2)\), \(s/(s^2+\omega^2)\) (§3.1).
Q2. Draw s-domain equivalent circuits of L and C including initial conditions. 5 marks
L: impedance \(sL\) in series with voltage \(L i(0^-)\), or parallel \(i(0^-)/s\). C: \(1/(sC)\) in series with \(v(0^-)/s\), or parallel current \(C v(0^-)\). State polarity carefully.
Q3. Using Laplace transform, find current in a series RL circuit switched to a DC source. 7 marks
\(I(s)=(V_s/s+Li_0)/(R+sL)\); partial fractions; invert (Example 1).
Q4. Solve series RC with exponential excitation by Laplace and verify against classical method. 7 marks
Voltage divider in \(s\)-domain → \(V_C(s)=V_m/[(RCs+1)(s+\alpha)]\); residues; inverse (Example 2).
Q5. Obtain \(i(t)\) and \(v_C(t)\) for series RLC under DC excitation (underdamped) using Laplace. 8 marks
\(I(s)=(V_0/L)/[(s+\alpha)^2+\omega_d^2]\); \(i(t)=(V_0/(L\omega_d))e^{-\alpha t}\sin\omega_d t\); then \(V_C=I/(sC)+v_C(0)/s\) (Example 4).
Q6. A source-free series RLC has nonzero \(i(0^-)\) and \(v_C(0^-)\). Find \(i(t)\) by Laplace. 7 marks
Numerator \(L i(0^-)-v_C(0^-)/s\); divide by \(R+sL+1/(sC)\); factor; partial fractions (Example 5).
Q7. Find the node voltage of a parallel RLC circuit for a DC current step using Laplace. 7 marks
\(V(s)=(I_0/C)/[s^2+(1/(RC))s+1/(LC)]\); invert with \(\alpha=1/(2RC)\) (Example 6).
Q8. Compare classical and Laplace methods for transient analysis. 4 marks
Use the comparison table: ODE vs algebra, when ICs enter, suitability for multi-loop switched circuits, need for inverse transform.
